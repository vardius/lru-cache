A `Cache` is safe for concurrent use: any number of goroutines can call its methods at once, with no lock of their own.

## One lock

Every method takes the cache's one `sync.Mutex`. `Get` takes it too, because a hit moves the key to the front of
the order of use: even reads change the cache, so a read-write lock would gain nothing.

The lock is held only for the cache's own work. Each method hashes its key before taking it, so the hashing of
one call overlaps the work of another, and a key that panics while being hashed never leaves the cache locked.

## Under load

A lock is cheap until goroutines wait for it. A hit costs {alone.ns.toFixed(1)} ns from one goroutine. In the read-through
benchmark, where 16 goroutines at once get keys and set those they miss, the cache gets through one lookup every
{loaded.ns.toFixed(0)} ns: that is wall time per call across all of them, so it measures throughput, not how long one call waits.

That is the price of waiting when goroutines do nothing but use the cache. When they do real work between calls,
such as handling a request or reading a file, they spend less of their time waiting.

<Aside type="caution" title="Shards change what is evicted">
Each shard evicts on its own. The entry that goes is the least recently used of its shard, not of the whole cache,
and one shard can be full while another has room, so a sharded cache can evict before it holds its full capacity.
Shard only when measurements show goroutines waiting on the cache.
</Aside>
